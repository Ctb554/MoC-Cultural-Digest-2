# Saudi Arabia/Regional

## Visual Arts:
- The Diriyah Biennale Foundation has named the four lead curators for the third Islamic Arts Biennale, opening at Jeddah's Western Hajj Terminal on 1 November 2027: Nada Raza of Dubai's Alserkal Arts Foundation, former Christie's Islamic art head William Robinson, Oxford professor Alain George, and Idries Trevathan, previously chief curator at Ithra in Dhahran. Assembling an internationally recognised curatorial team around Islamic art and intangible heritage strengthens the Kingdom's positioning as a centre for serious scholarship, not only spectacle, well ahead of the event itself. ([ArtReview](https://artreview.com/islamic-arts-biennale-2027-names-lead-curators/))
- Jeddah-based artist Dana Awartani, of Saudi and Palestinian descent, has been named to the inaugural 2026 TIME100 Art list alongside figures such as Banksy and Tracey Emin, cited for immersive installations that memorialise heritage sites damaged by conflict, including the 29,000 handmade clay bricks she laid across Saudi Arabia's Venice Biennale pavilion. Recognition of a Jeddah-based artist on a global influence list reflects the growing critical standing of the Kingdom's contemporary art scene beyond its own institutions. ([TIME](https://time.com/collection/time100-art/2026/dana-awartani/))

## Film:
- Saudi Film Nights will make its South African debut from 8-14 October, screening Saudi features and shorts in Johannesburg, Durban and Cape Town, presented by the Joburg Film Festival on behalf of the Saudi Film Commission with post-screening Q&As from attending Saudi filmmakers. The tour extends the Kingdom's film-sector soft power into a new market and signals a deliberate push to build South-South cultural ties around Saudi cinema. ([MDNtv](https://mdntvlive.com/saudi-film-nights-makes-historic-south-african-debut-this-october/))
- The Dubai International Film Festival is being revived for December 2027 after a near-decade hiatus, with trade coverage noting that the 2026 edition of Saudi Arabia's Red Sea International Film Festival, widely credited with filling the void Dubai left, now looks uncertain. Renewed regional competition for festival prestige is a reminder that the Red Sea Festival's momentum depends on scheduling clarity, and that the Kingdom's lead in Gulf film events is no longer uncontested. ([Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/09/dubai-film-festival-return-saudi-red-sea-film-uncertain-1237106423/))

The find shows how non-invasive digital survey techniques are reshaping heritage research, the same class of technology now central to documenting and interpreting Saudi Arabia's own archaeological sites. ([Arkeonews](https://arkeonews.net/new-laser-scans-reveal-six-groups-of-ancient-ships-on-a-3000-year-old-wall-in-cyprus/))

Such discoveries sustain the global public appetite for archaeology and heritage storytelling, the very interest Saudi Arabia is cultivating around sites such as AlUla and Hegra. # Risks and Opportunities

## Risks

1. **Red Sea and southern-border security escalation**
A sharp escalation between Saudi Arabia and Yemen's Houthis this week, with civilian casualties across the Kingdom's south, a claimed downing of a Saudi jet, and an attack that forced the closure of the East-West oil pipeline and rattled crude markets, has widened the reputational and economic backdrop against which the Ministry's work is read. Regional outlets also applied adversarial framing to the Kingdom's role in Yemen, foregrounding civilian harm.
Source: [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/17/five-killed-as-saudi-arabia-and-yemens-houthis-trade-attacks), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/17/oil-prices-today-wti-brent-hormuz-iran-war.html)
Consideration: Keep the Ministry's cultural programming visibly distinct from the security story by foregrounding credible, on-schedule delivery, public access and safe international participation. The Islamic Arts Biennale's international curatorial team and Saudi Film Nights' overseas tour are exactly the kind of steady, apolitical cultural signals worth amplifying now.

2. **Arms and nuclear scrutiny drawing critical attention**
The US approval of a 24.3-billion-dollar F-35 package and a Saudi announcement of a uranium find near Medina landed in the same week, each attracting scrutiny: intelligence, congressional and allied concerns over the jets, and questions over enrichment intentions on the nuclear side. Together they reinforce a security-and-strategic-hedging image of the Kingdom that can crowd out its cultural story in international coverage.
Source: [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/17/trump-administration-approves-sale-of-f-35-jets-to-saudi-arabia), [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/09/17/2026/saudi-arabias-well-timed-uranium-find-near-medina)
Consideration: Maintain a clear separation between defence and geopolitical headlines and the Ministry's cultural-diplomacy agenda, leaning into heritage stewardship, genuine cultural exchange and institutional professionalism as the Kingdom's soft-power counterweight.

## Opportunities

1. **Saudi cultural institutions gaining international curatorial and critical stature**
Two signals this week point to rising external validation of Saudi cultural institutions: the Diriyah Biennale Foundation's appointment of a senior international curatorial team for the 2027 Islamic Arts Biennale, and Jeddah-based artist Dana Awartani's inclusion on the inaugural TIME100 Art list. Both place Saudi-linked cultural work within global critical conversations rather than at their margins.
Source: [ArtReview](https://artreview.com/islamic-arts-biennale-2027-names-lead-curators/), [TIME](https://time.com/collection/time100-art/2026/dana-awartani/)
Consideration: Connect these into a single national narrative of credible, internationally respected cultural institutions and talent, emphasising that recognition is being earned on artistic and scholarly merit, to build durable soft-power capital.

2. **Saudi cinema extending its international reach**
Saudi cinema's international footprint featured twice this cycle: the Saudi Film Commission's first Saudi Film Nights tour of South Africa, and renewed regional festival competition as Dubai revives its film festival amid uncertainty over the Red Sea Festival's 2026 edition. The first shows the Kingdom actively exporting its film culture; the second is a prompt to protect the Red Sea Festival's hard-won standing.
Source: [MDNtv](https://mdntvlive.com/saudi-film-nights-makes-historic-south-african-debut-this-october/), [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/09/dubai-film-festival-return-saudi-red-sea-film-uncertain-1237106423/)
Consideration: Sustain film-sector momentum through talent pathways and international showcases like the South Africa tour, while giving early clarity on the Red Sea International Film Festival's calendar to reassure the industry and preserve the Kingdom's lead in Gulf film events.
