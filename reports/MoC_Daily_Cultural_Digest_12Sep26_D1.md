# Saudi Arabia/Regional

## General:
- The inaugural Lovin' Riyadh Awards have unveiled the nominees for their "Pillar of Culture" category, spotlighting a slate of Saudi content creators and community voices shaping the capital's cultural conversation, as the regional Lovin network extends its audience-voted awards format to Riyadh for the first time. The recognition of grassroots cultural figures — rather than only institutions or official programming — points to a maturing bottom-up cultural identity in the capital that complements the Ministry's more formal initiatives, and offers a read on how everyday cultural life in Riyadh is being narrated by Saudis themselves. ([Lovin Riyadh](https://lovin.co/riyadh/en/best-of/the-first-ever-lovin-riyadh-awards-meet-the-pillar-of-culture-nominees))

For the Ministry, the event's framing of culture and heritage as central assets of the Kingdom's tourism proposition reinforces how cultural stewardship is being positioned within the wider visitor-economy agenda, even as the specific focus here is the commercial travel trade rather than cultural programming itself. ([Travel and Tour World](https://www.travelandtourworld.com/news/article/y9e1cvjn4ati/))

The debate is a useful reference point for the Ministry as it weighs sustainable financing models for culture, illustrating how other governments are trying to convert tourism revenue into durable arts investment. ## Heritage:
- Archaeologists have uncovered a vast 300-metre Iron Age mining complex beneath Hallstatt, the Austrian site whose ancient salt industry gives its name to a whole era of European prehistory, in a find that deepens understanding of early industrial-scale extraction. Discoveries of this scale at long-studied heritage sites show how active archaeological research continually reframes even well-known destinations, a dynamic directly relevant to the Kingdom's own heavily programmed sites such as AlUla and Hegra. ([Arkeonews](https://arkeonews.net/vast-300-meter-iron-age-mining-complex-discovered-beneath-hallstatt))
- Archaeology Magazine reports that bioarchaeologists studying Indonesian hunter-gatherer remains have found evidence that betel nuts may have been used to treat toothaches thousands of years ago, adding to the record of ancient medicinal practice. Fine-grained studies like this underline how intangible heritage — traditional knowledge of plants, remedies and daily life — is increasingly recoverable through science, a frame the Kingdom can apply to its own research into historic Arabian lifeways. ([Archaeology Magazine](https://archaeology.org/news/2026/09/11/indonesian-hunter-gatherers-may-have-treated-toothaches-with-betel-nuts))

# Risks and Opportunities

## Risks

1. **Red Sea security escalation deepens the reputational backdrop**
The sharpest reputational pressure this cycle again comes not from culture but from a further escalation on Saudi Arabia's southern flank, intensifying the same Red Sea security risk flagged in recent editions. Saudi Arabia has shut its East-West crude pipeline after drone strikes reportedly launched from Iraq, Houthi forces advancing along Yemen's Red Sea coast are threatening the Kingdom's oil exports and the Bab al-Mandeb chokepoint, and commentary casts the leadership as strategically cornered, together throwing a security shadow over the same period in which the Kingdom is projecting an open, culture-led international image.
Source: [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/11/saudi-arabia-shut-down-east-west-crude-oil-pipeline.html), [Nikkei Asia](https://asia.nikkei.com/spotlight/iran-tensions/iran-war/houthis-advance-along-yemeni-coast-threaten-saudi-red-sea-oil-exports), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-11/houthi-gains-leave-mbs-with-few-good-options-in-yemen)
Consideration: As in recent editions, the Ministry should keep its cultural narrative deliberately distinct from the conflict, avoiding any framing that ties heritage or arts programming to the security posture, while continuing to foreground credible delivery, public access, Saudi talent and safe international cultural exchange as the substance of the Kingdom's global cultural presence.

2. **Renewed 9/11-linked pressure targets cultural soft power**
Separately, the 25th anniversary of the 11 September attacks has revived a long-running reputational vector, with a 9/11 families campaigner publicly criticising Saudi Arabia at the commemoration and specifically urging Hollywood to boycott the Kingdom's Red Sea International Film Festival in December. Because the campaign takes direct aim at a flagship cultural event and its international participation, it converts a political-legacy issue into a cultural-soft-power risk bearing on one of the Kingdom's marquee cultural platforms.
Source: [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/11/911-widow-saudi-arabia-trump), [Showbiz411](https://www.showbiz411.com/2026/09/11/9-11-activist-terry-strada-speaks-out-against-saudi-arabia-will-hollywood-remember-in-december-and-skip-red-sea-festival)
Consideration: The Ministry can protect the festival's international standing by foregrounding its substantive programming, Saudi filmmaking talent and value to visiting industry professionals, letting the festival's cultural and creative merits — rather than political rebuttal — carry its reputation with international participants.

## Opportunities

1. **Cultural tourism and grassroots identity build momentum ahead of National Day**
Two otherwise separate stories point the same way as the Kingdom approaches National Day on 23 September: the inaugural WTM Spotlight Riyadh set out strategies to grow a travel market in which culture and heritage are treated as central assets, while the first Lovin' Riyadh Awards spotlighted grassroots Saudi cultural creators shaping the capital's identity from the bottom up. Read together, they show the Kingdom's cultural offer developing on two fronts at once, institutional and commercial visitor-economy positioning alongside organic, community-led cultural life.
Source: [Travel and Tour World](https://www.travelandtourworld.com/news/article/y9e1cvjn4ati/), [Lovin Riyadh](https://lovin.co/riyadh/en/best-of/the-first-ever-lovin-riyadh-awards-meet-the-pillar-of-culture-nominees)
Consideration: The Ministry can connect these threads into a single National Day narrative — that Saudi culture is both a driver of the visitor economy and a living, citizen-led identity — using the national-day moment to link formal programming with the grassroots cultural energy that gives it authenticity.
